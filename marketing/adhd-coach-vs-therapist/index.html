ADHD Coach vs. Therapist vs. Apps: Which Do You Need?

Therapists treat ADHD. Coaches help you run your days. Apps remind you — if you open them. Many adults with ADHD have medication and therapy in place and still can’t start the email on Tuesday afternoon. That execution gap — planning, starting, following through — is what coaching exists to close, and it’s a different job from treatment.

Updated August 30, 2026

What an ADHD therapist or psychiatrist does

Licensed clinicians diagnose ADHD, prescribe and manage medication and treat what travels with it — anxiety, depression, shame. Therapy looks backward and inward as well as forward, and it is the right front door if you are undiagnosed, in crisis or struggling with mood. Sessions are typically weekly and sometimes insurance-covered.

What an ADHD coach does

Coaching is present, forward-looking and practical: building routines, breaking tasks down, external accountability, follow-through. Coaches are not licensed clinicians and coaching is not covered by insurance — so any provider’s training and clinical oversight are worth asking about. Traditional coaches charge $75–$250 an hour for one session a week (full cost breakdown here).

What apps do

Task managers and reminder apps are cheap (free to ~$20/month) and genuinely useful for capture and scheduling. Their limit is structural: an app can’t notice you’re avoiding something, can’t care whether you show up, and is one swipe from dismissed. For most ADHD adults, apps organize the work; they don’t start it. Everything an app does is already inside Get Up and Flow, in richer form: reminders arrive curated by your coach — a clear title, the context you’ll want in your head, any files you’ll need — with a human making sure the follow-through actually happens.

How they fit together

Psychiatry and psychology are often necessary, and coaching is built to complement them: treatment stabilizes the brain while coaching builds the daily scaffolding. Apps are a different story — task lists and reminders are part of our service, delivered in richer, human-curated form, so for most clients we largely replace apps and meaningfully improve on them. Our coaches — most holding postgraduate psychology degrees, many of them clinical psychologists with real ADHD-facing practice experience — train on the proprietary curriculum, methodologies and supporting technology our founders spent a year researching and refining: an intensive training program up front, then continuous training with ongoing quality review by Dr. Parsons and the clinical team. Strict protocol boundaries keep coaching out of intensive psychotherapy, and clear escalation protocols route anything clinical to licensed clinicians. Coaching with us is a complement to treatment, never a replacement for it.

A simple way to choose

Undiagnosed, in crisis or wrestling with mood — start with a clinician. Diagnosed and stable but the days still fall apart — that’s coaching.
