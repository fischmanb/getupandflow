A Day With Your ADHD Accountability Coach

All-day coaching is four touchpoints wrapped around your actual workday: a morning Jump Start, reminders at the moments you chose, live Panic Button help when you stall, and an optional end-of-day Retro. Same coach every day, 8am–5pm, Monday through Friday. Here is what that looks like in practice.

Updated August 30, 2026

8:00 — Jump Start: never stare at a blank day

You start the day on a short call with your coach — the same person as yesterday, who already knows what’s on your plate. Together you lay out the day, pick the first task, and break it small enough to actually begin. Task initiation is the hardest executive function for most ADHD adults; the Jump Start exists so the day starts with momentum instead of a staring contest with the to-do list.

Through the day — reminders at your commitment points

Your coach checks in over WhatsApp or text at the moments you committed to in the morning — not generic nudges, but “you said the report draft by 11, how’s it going?” A reminder from a person who will notice the answer lands differently than a notification you can swipe away.

When you stall — the Panic Button

It’s 2pm and the email still isn’t sent. Instead of spiraling, you hit the Panic Button and your coach gets you live — up to 45 minutes a day of real-time help to untangle the block, shrink the task, and sit with you while you start. This is the moment weekly coaching structurally can’t reach: the stall happens on Tuesday, not in Thursday’s session.

4:45 — optional Retro: keep what worked

A short optional wrap-up: what got done, what worked, what to carry into tomorrow. No grades, no shame — the point is that tomorrow’s Jump Start starts smarter than today’s.

Why the same coach matters

Every touchpoint compounds because it’s one person: your coach learns your patterns, your avoidance tells, and what actually gets you moving. That continuity is the difference between a service and a partner — and it’s why we never rotate coaches.
