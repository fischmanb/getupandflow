How Much Does an ADHD Coach Cost?

ADHD coaching typically costs $75–$250 per hour in the United States in 2026. Most coaches meet clients once a week for 30–60 minutes, with one planned text or email check-in between sessions. Monthly packages generally run $300–$750, and insurance does not cover ADHD coaching.

Updated August 30, 2026

ADHD coaching hourly rates in 2026

Independent guides from Understood.org and the coach directory Noomii both place typical rates at $75–$200 per hour. Coach-training and certification sources report $100–$250 per session, with newer coaches starting at $75–$125 and experienced, PCAC-credentialed coaches charging $175–$250 or more. ADDitude Magazine puts average monthly cost at $300–$600, with some arrangements reaching $1,500 per month.

What you actually get for that price

The standard unit of ADHD coaching is one 30–60 minute session per week plus one client-initiated check-in between sessions. Subscription services price similarly per unit of coach time: Shimmer, for example, charges $230 per month for one 30-minute video session per week with text messaging in between. Whatever the wrapper, most of the week — the Tuesday 2pm moment when the email still isn’t sent — falls outside what you’ve bought.

What all-day coaching coverage would cost at hourly rates

A useful way to compare offerings is coverage: how many hours a month is a coach actually available to you? Full workday coverage — 9 hours a day, Monday through Friday — is about 195 coach-hours per month. At market hourly rates, that coverage would cost:

Does insurance cover ADHD coaching?

No. ADHD coaching is not covered by insurance and is paid out of pocket. Coaching is also not therapy or medical care: coaches are not licensed clinicians — which is why credentials, training, and clinical oversight are worth asking any provider about.
